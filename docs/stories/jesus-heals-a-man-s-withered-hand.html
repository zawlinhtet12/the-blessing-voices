Miracles

Jesus Heals a Man’s Withered Hand

Reflect on Jesus’ teaching about doing good on the Sabbath as he restores a man’s hand.

Matthew 12:9–14; Mark 3:1–6; Luke 6:6–11

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the Gospel account alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

Jesus entered a synagogue where there was a man with a withered hand. People asked whether it was lawful to heal on the Sabbath, seeking grounds to accuse him. Jesus spoke of rescuing a sheep from a pit on the Sabbath and affirmed the greater value of a person. He declared that it was lawful to do good on the Sabbath. He told the man to stretch out his hand, and it was restored. The Pharisees went out and plotted against Jesus. This paraphrase follows Matthew 12:9–14.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Mark 3:1–6 and Luke 6:6–11 provide parallel accounts. The dispute concerns doing good on the Sabbath. The story reports particular opponents and should not be used to stereotype Jewish people or Judaism.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The passage invites reflection on compassion, the value of a person, and the choice to do good. The man’s need remains central even amid a dispute.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Consider whether habit or fear of criticism is keeping you from a thoughtful act of kindness. Make community activities more welcoming and accessible to people with disabilities.

Reflection Questions

  1. How does Jesus respond to the question about Sabbath healing?
  2. What does his example of a sheep help illustrate?
  3. What practical good can you do for someone this week?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for your compassion. Help us trust you, listen carefully to your teaching, and care for others with patience, kindness, and respect. Amen.
