The Blind Leading the Blind

Consider Jesus’ warning about unreliable guidance and the need for discernment.

Matthew 15:14; Luke 6:39–40

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical teaching alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

Jesus used the image of one blind person guiding another and asked whether both would fall into a pit. In Luke, this is followed by teaching about a disciple and a teacher. This paraphrase reflects Matthew 15:14 and Luke 6:39–40.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Matthew places the image in a dispute about teaching and defilement; Luke includes it among teachings to disciples. The illustration concerns unreliable guidance and should not be used to demean people with visual disabilities.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The saying invites discernment about whom we follow and humility about the guidance we offer. A teacher’s influence makes careful learning important.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Check advice against Scripture in context. Be honest about the limits of your understanding and seek wise help before guiding someone else.

Reflection Questions

  1. What danger does the illustration describe?
  2. How does a teacher’s influence affect a learner?
  3. Where do you need greater humility or discernment in giving advice?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help us understand your Word and receive it with humility. Guide our choices and teach us to live with faith, wisdom, and love. Amen.
