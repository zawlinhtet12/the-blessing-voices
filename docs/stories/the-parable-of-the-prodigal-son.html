Parables & Metaphors

The Parable of the Prodigal Son

Explore a father’s welcome, a younger son’s return, and an older brother’s struggle to share the joy.

Luke 15:11–32

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical passage alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

A man’s younger son asked for his share of the property, left for a distant country, and squandered it. When famine came, he became destitute and tended pigs. He decided to return, confess his sin, and ask to be treated as a hired worker. His father saw him coming, ran to him, embraced him, and welcomed him with a robe, ring, sandals, and a feast. The older son became angry and refused to join the celebration. His father went out to plead with him, affirming their relationship and explaining the joy of receiving back a brother who had been lost. This is a paraphrase of Luke 15:11–32.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Often called the Prodigal Son, the story includes two sons and their father. It does not report the older son’s final response to his father’s appeal.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The story invites reflection on repentance, generous welcome, resentment, and the invitation to share joy over restoration.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Consider where you need to return, apologize, or welcome someone. Examine resentment when another person receives kindness.

Reflection Questions

  1. What leads the younger son to return?
  2. How does the father respond to each son?
  3. Where might you need to receive or extend a gracious welcome?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help us hear your Word with humility. Guide our choices and teach us to respond with faith, mercy, and faithful action. Amen.
