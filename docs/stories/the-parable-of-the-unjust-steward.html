The Parable of the Unjust Steward

Consider the dishonest manager’s urgent planning and Jesus’ teaching about faithful stewardship.

Luke 16:1–13

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical passage alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

A rich man heard that his manager was wasting his possessions and required an account. Facing dismissal, the manager reduced the bills of his master’s debtors so that they might welcome him later. One bill for oil was reduced from a hundred measures to fifty; a wheat bill was reduced from a hundred to eighty. The master commended the dishonest manager’s shrewdness. Jesus then taught about the use of wealth, faithfulness in small matters, and the impossibility of serving both God and money. This is a paraphrase of Luke 16:1–13.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The manager is explicitly called dishonest. The commendation concerns his shrewd response, not an instruction for disciples to commit fraud. Jesus’ following sayings about faithfulness help frame the difficult illustration.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The passage invites serious attention to stewardship, foresight, and loyalty to God rather than money.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Review how you handle resources entrusted to you. Correct one careless habit and choose a generous, honest use of money or time.

Reflection Questions

  1. What problem prompts the manager’s plan?
  2. Why must shrewdness be distinguished from dishonesty?
  3. How can you be more faithful with something entrusted to you?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help us hear your Word with humility. Guide our choices and teach us to respond with faith, mercy, and faithful action. Amen.
