The Parable of the Unforgiving Servant

Explore the contrast between receiving great mercy and refusing to show mercy to someone else.

Matthew 18:23–35

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical account alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and the closing prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

A king began settling accounts with his servants. One owed ten thousand talents and could not repay. After the servant pleaded for patience, the king had compassion, released him, and forgave the debt. That servant then found a fellow servant who owed him a hundred denarii. He seized him by the throat and demanded repayment. Although the other man pleaded for patience, he refused and had him imprisoned. Other servants reported what had happened. The king summoned the servant and asked why he had not shown the same mercy he had received. In anger, he handed him over to be tortured until the debt was repaid. Jesus closed with a serious warning about forgiving a brother or sister from the heart. This is a paraphrase of Matthew 18:23–35.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The parable contrasts an immense debt forgiven by the king with a much smaller debt demanded from a fellow servant. Its focus is the servant’s refusal to extend mercy after receiving it. Jesus’ concluding warning makes forgiveness a matter of the heart, not simply outward words.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The booklet highlights God’s forgiveness, the call to forgive others, and the seriousness of refusing mercy. It connects these themes with Ephesians 1:7, Colossians 3:13, and Matthew 6:14–15. Those readings support devotional study and are separate from the parable.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Reflect on the mercy you have received and bring resentment to God in prayer. Consider a concrete step away from retaliation and toward forgiveness. Seek wise support in difficult relationships; a devotional response should be thoughtful and sincere.

Reflection Questions

  1. What is striking about the difference between the two debts?
  2. How should receiving mercy affect the way you treat others?
  3. What step toward forgiveness could you bring to God this week?

Closing Prayer

Merciful God, thank you for your forgiveness. Soften our hearts where resentment has taken root and teach us to extend mercy with sincerity and wisdom. Amen.
