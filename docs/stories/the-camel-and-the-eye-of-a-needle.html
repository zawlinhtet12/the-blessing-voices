Parables & Metaphors

The Camel and the Eye of a Needle

Reflect on wealth, dependence on God, and Jesus’ striking picture of a camel and a needle.

Matthew 19:24–26; Mark 10:25–27; Luke 18:25–27

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical teaching alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

Jesus said it was easier for a camel to pass through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter God’s kingdom. When the disciples asked who could be saved, Jesus pointed to what is impossible for people being possible with God. This paraphrase follows Matthew 19:24–26, with related accounts in Mark 10:25–27 and Luke 18:25–27.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The saying follows Jesus’ encounter with a wealthy man. The text names a needle’s eye; this study does not add an unmentioned city gate or an invented account of camels unloading baggage.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The striking contrast challenges confidence in wealth and directs attention to dependence on God. The disciples’ question and Jesus’ answer are essential to reading it.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Examine whether possessions are becoming a source of ultimate security. Practise generosity and reflect on your dependence on God.

Reflection Questions

  1. Why are the disciples astonished by this saying?
  2. How does Jesus answer their question about salvation?
  3. How could generosity help you reconsider your attachment to possessions?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help us understand your Word and receive it with humility. Guide our choices and teach us to live with faith, wisdom, and love. Amen.
