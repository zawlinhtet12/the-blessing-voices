Jesus Heals a Woman Crippled for Eighteen Years

Consider Jesus’ care for a woman bent over for eighteen years and his defence of her freedom on the Sabbath.

Luke 13:10–17

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the Gospel account alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

While teaching in a synagogue on the Sabbath, Jesus saw a woman who had been bent over for eighteen years and could not straighten herself. He called her forward, declared her freed from her infirmity, and laid his hands on her. She immediately stood straight and praised God. The synagogue leader objected to healing on the Sabbath. Jesus replied that people untied their animals to give them water on the Sabbath, and spoke of this daughter of Abraham being released from her bondage. His opponents were put to shame, while the crowd rejoiced. This is a paraphrase of Luke 13:10–17.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Luke presents the woman’s healing within a Sabbath dispute. Jesus calls her a daughter of Abraham, affirming her place within the community. The passage describes this woman’s situation rather than offering a general diagnosis for disability.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The account invites reflection on compassion, liberation, dignity, and praise. Jesus notices someone who has endured suffering and defends the appropriateness of helping her.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Notice people who have experienced exclusion or long-term difficulty. Ask how you can support their participation in community life. Offer care without making assumptions about the cause of someone’s disability.

Reflection Questions

  1. How does Jesus respond when he sees the woman?
  2. Why is his description of her as a daughter of Abraham significant?
  3. What could you do to help someone feel recognized and included?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for your compassion. Help us trust you, listen carefully to your teaching, and care for others with patience, kindness, and respect. Amen.
