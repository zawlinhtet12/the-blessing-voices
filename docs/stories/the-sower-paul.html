Parables & Metaphors

The Sower (Paul)

Explore sowing and reaping as an image of choices, perseverance, and life in the Spirit.

Galatians 6:7–9

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical teaching alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

Paul warns that a person reaps what they sow. He contrasts sowing to the flesh, with its harvest of corruption, and sowing to the Spirit, with its harvest of eternal life. He encourages believers not to grow weary in doing good, because they will reap at the proper time if they do not give up. This is a paraphrase of Galatians 6:7–9.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

This is Paul’s sowing metaphor in Galatians, distinct from Jesus’ Parable of the Sower and its four soils. It does not provide a formula promising financial returns from gifts.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Choices have consequences, and perseverance in doing good matters. The contrast is shaped by Paul’s teaching about the Spirit.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Examine a habit you are cultivating. Choose a small act of goodness you can sustain, even when results are not immediately visible.

Reflection Questions

  1. What two kinds of sowing does Paul contrast?
  2. What encouragement does he give to those growing weary?
  3. What habit would you like to cultivate more faithfully?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help us receive your Word with humility and put it into practice. Shape our words, choices, and relationships with truth, faithfulness, and love. Amen.
