The Parable of the Rich Man and Lazarus

Reflect on neglected suffering, a reversal after death, and the call to listen to Scripture.

Luke 16:19–31

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical passage alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

A rich man lived in luxury while Lazarus, a poor man covered in sores, lay at his gate longing for food. Both died. Lazarus was carried to Abraham’s side, while the rich man was in torment. The rich man asked for relief, but Abraham spoke of their changed circumstances and a fixed chasm between them. The rich man then asked that Lazarus warn his five brothers. Abraham replied that they had Moses and the prophets to hear, and that refusing to listen to them would not be overcome even by someone rising from the dead. This is a paraphrase of Luke 16:19–31.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

This Lazarus is a character in Jesus’ story, distinct from Lazarus of Bethany in John 11. The rich man is not named in Luke’s text.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The account challenges indifference to suffering and emphasizes the seriousness of hearing Scripture. Its imagery should be read within this story rather than expanded with invented details.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Notice a need close to you that comfort has made easy to ignore. Respond with practical generosity and attentive listening to Scripture.

Reflection Questions

  1. What contrast does the opening scene establish?
  2. What does Abraham say the brothers should hear?
  3. Whose need near you deserves greater attention?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help us hear your Word with humility. Guide our choices and teach us to respond with faith, mercy, and faithful action. Amen.
