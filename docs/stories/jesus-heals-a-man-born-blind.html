Jesus Heals a Man Born Blind

Explore the healing, testimony, and growing recognition of Jesus in the account of a man born blind.

John 9:1–41

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the Gospel account alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

Jesus saw a man blind from birth. When the disciples asked whether his blindness resulted from his own sin or his parents’ sin, Jesus rejected those alternatives and spoke of God’s works being displayed. He made mud, put it on the man’s eyes, and told him to wash in the pool of Siloam. The man went, washed, and returned seeing. Neighbours and religious authorities questioned him. He spoke about what had happened despite opposition. After he was cast out, Jesus found him, revealed himself as the Son of Man, and received his belief and worship. Jesus then spoke about sight and blindness. This is a paraphrase of John 9:1–41.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

John’s extended account moves from physical healing to testimony, opposition, and recognition of Jesus. It does not justify blaming a disabled person or their parents for disability. The language of spiritual sight belongs to the passage’s concluding discussion.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The narrative invites reflection on truthful testimony, humility, and willingness to recognize Jesus. The man describes what he knows rather than pretending to answer every question.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Listen to another person’s own account of their experience. Reject simplistic blame for suffering. Speak honestly about what you know and remain willing to learn.

Reflection Questions

  1. How does Jesus answer the disciples’ opening question?
  2. How does the man respond when questioned about the healing?
  3. Where could greater humility and attentive listening shape your response to someone else?

Closing Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for your mercy. Help us listen to your teaching and respond to others with gratitude, patience, and compassion. Amen.
