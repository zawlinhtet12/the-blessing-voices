Cloth and Wineskins

Consider Jesus’ paired illustrations of a new patch and new wine.

Matthew 9:16–17; Mark 2:21–22; Luke 5:36–39

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical teaching alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

Jesus explained that an unshrunk patch on an old garment could pull away and make the tear worse. He also described new wine bursting old wineskins, with both wine and skins lost. New wine belongs in fresh wineskins so that both are preserved. This paraphrase follows Matthew 9:16–17.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

These illustrations occur in a discussion about fasting. Mark 2:21–22 and Luke 5:36–39 provide related accounts; Luke includes a further saying about preference for old wine. Read these as teaching images rather than as a blanket dismissal of everything old.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The paired comparisons invite thoughtful attention to the significance of Jesus’ ministry and the fit between what is received and how it is held. Interpretive applications should remain distinct from the images themselves.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Examine a habit that may need to change as you learn from Jesus. Approach change thoughtfully without assuming that novelty alone makes something faithful.

Reflection Questions

  1. What goes wrong with the patch in the illustration?
  2. What happens when new wine is put in old skins?
  3. Where might attentive learning call for a change in your habits?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help us understand your Word and receive it with humility. Guide our choices and teach us to live with faith, wisdom, and love. Amen.
