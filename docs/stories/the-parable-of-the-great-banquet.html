The Parable of the Great Banquet

Reflect on the invitation to a great banquet and the excuses that keep people from responding.

Luke 14:15–24

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical passage alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

A man prepared a great banquet and invited many guests. When it was ready, he sent his servant to summon them. They offered excuses about a field, oxen, and a recent marriage. The host then sent his servant into the streets and lanes to bring in people who were poor or disabled. When room remained, he sent the servant farther out so that the house would be filled. He declared that those originally invited would not taste his banquet. This is a paraphrase of Luke 14:15–24.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Jesus tells this story in the setting of a meal. It is distinct from Matthew’s wedding-banquet parable; Luke’s account does not call this a wedding feast.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The parable invites reflection on responding to God’s invitation, misplaced priorities, and hospitality that includes people who are overlooked.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Consider what excuses crowd out your response to God. Make room at a meal or community activity for someone who might otherwise be excluded.

Reflection Questions

  1. What excuses do the invited guests give?
  2. Whom does the host invite next?
  3. What practical change could help you respond to God’s invitation?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help us hear your Word with humility. Guide our choices and teach us to respond with faith, mercy, and faithful action. Amen.
