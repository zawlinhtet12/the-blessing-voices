The Strong Man

Explore the strong-man illustration in Jesus’ response to accusations about his authority.

Matthew 12:29; Mark 3:27; Luke 11:21–22

Introduction

Read this English paraphrase of the biblical teaching alongside the original companion booklet. Context notes, devotional applications, reflection questions, and prayer are study aids rather than additional biblical text.

The Bible Story

Jesus asked how anyone could enter a strong man’s house and carry off his possessions without first binding the strong man. Only then could the house be plundered. This paraphrase follows Matthew 12:29, with related wording in Mark 3:27.

Historical and Biblical Context

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

The saying appears in Jesus’ response to accusations about casting out demons. Luke 11:21–22 uses a related illustration of a stronger opponent overcoming an armed guard. These are images within an argument about authority, not instructions for burglary or violence.

Spiritual Lessons

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Read within its context, the illustration points to power overcoming an opposing power and invites reflection on Jesus’ authority. It should not become a story with invented named combatants.

Practical Life Applications

CONTEXT & DEVOTIONAL INTERPRETATION

Read the surrounding argument before drawing conclusions from the image. Reflect on how trust in Jesus can shape a faithful response to fear.

Reflection Questions

  1. What must happen before the strong man’s house can be plundered?
  2. How does the surrounding dispute help explain the image?
  3. How can reading a saying in context improve your understanding?

Closing Prayer

Lord God, help us understand your Word and receive it with humility. Guide our choices and teach us to live with faith, wisdom, and love. Amen.
